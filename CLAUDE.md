## Project Context

A parser laboratory disguised as a small house game. Six rooms, seven puzzles, ten points, and a family locket at the end. Every puzzle exists to test a verb pattern: look under, search, push, light, open with a known combination, dig, put in, wind. The point of the game is that whatever natural phrasing a first-time player reaches for should do something sensible, so the story carries about fifty synonym patterns on the standard verbs and a dozen story verbs of its own. The Inform 7 sibling lives in `C:/code/text-games/i7/guess-the-verb-inform7/`.

## Design Documents

The story file is the design record. `legacy/src/` holds the TypeScript original: `grammar.ts` is the list of phrasings the game promised to understand, and `legacy/tests/` its three transcripts (naive playthrough, verb synonyms, unconventional commands) plus the walkthrough. Keep them until the Chord edition has shipped and been played.

## Writing Style Rules

- Plain, friendly, second person. Puzzle responses say what happened and, on refusals, what to try instead.
- Hints are allowed and in-world where possible ("Try going UP to climb the stairs.").
- Room descriptions name their exits; this game predates the workspace style rule.

## Key Design Decisions (do not change without discussion)

- Synonyms of standard verbs are `extend action` grammar lines (examining, taking, looking, inventory, opening, pushing, pulling, dropping, searching, attacking, eating, wearing, inserting, digging). Story verbs (peeking, ringing, knocking, lighting, dialing, winding, repairing, combining, greeting, helping, listing-verbs) dispatch through traits on the things they are for.
- A story verb must not restate a standard verb's own pattern for everything: `turn the target` under `dialing` stole the kitchen faucet. Turning the safe's dial is the standard `turning` with an entity clause on the safe.
- The combination is never typed. Chord 3.6 has no working free-text slot, so the safe opens with OPEN SAFE (or DIAL SAFE, TURN DIAL) once the lit fireplace has shown 7-3-9. The TypeScript edition accepted `dial 7 3 9` as well as this auto-open path.
- Things the player must not carry refuse with their own text in `on the player taking`; per-entity `phrase <alias>:` overrides of standard messages compiled but had no effect at 3.6.0.
- The doormat is portable: taking it commits, the `after` clause reveals the key and puts the mat back. That is the only way a standard verb on a fixed thing can mutate the world, because a refused action discards the clause's changes.
- The wall safe starts out of play and is moved into the Study when the painting is pushed, pulled, or looked behind. The trunk's lock is a story state (`latched`, `released`) gated in `on the player opening`; winding the music box releases it.
- All eight scores are declared in the story header so any clause can award them. Total 10: key, matchbook, fireplace, safe, mechanism, winding key, music box (2), locket (2). Taking the locket wins.
